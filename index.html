01 FREGATA

Named for the bird
that barely lands.

The frigatebird stays aloft over open sea for days on a long, efficient wing. FREGATA is built on the same idea: lift off without a runway, then ride a long wing for hours while ten small electric propellers rest.

02 Technology

One wing.
Three ways to fly.

The body stays level the whole time. The wing and the tail crossbar turn together, from pointing at the sky to lying flat, and the flight software holds the aircraft steady through every degree.

  1. 01 · Lift

    Straight up, off the deck

    Wing on edge, all ten propellers pointing at the sky. The battery helps the diesel through the few hungry minutes of vertical flight.

  2. 02 · Transition

    Tilt into the wind

    Wing and tail tilt forward together as speed builds, until the wing carries the weight. This is the hardest moment for a tilt-wing, and the one we simulate most.

  3. 03 · Loiter

    Fold eight, fly on two

    Eight propellers fold flat against their pods. The two at the wingtips pull FREGATA along on a few kilowatts, and the diesel recharges the battery.

A hybrid heart.

Batteries alone cannot fly for a day, and engines alone cannot hover efficiently. FREGATA carries both, and the software decides which one works when.

  • Hybrid powerSmall heavy-fuel diesels turn alternators. They run the aircraft in cruise and recharge the battery.
  • BatteryCovers takeoff, landing and the transition, when power demand peaks.
  • Ten motorsDistributed along the wing and tail. If one stops, the others make up the difference.
  • Flight computerAutopilot, safety checks and the radio link, with a backup.
  • Sensor ballA swappable pod under the nose: day and thermal cameras looking down and out to sea.
  • One wiring spinePower and data with blind-mate plugs at every module joint.

Flown by Galleion

The software is the aircraft's other half.

Galleion is where FREGATA is designed, simulated, flown and kept: flight software for the hover-to-wing transition, the fence and beyond-sight operations, ground control, relief planning, maintenance for every module and a signed record of every sortie.

03 Missions

Always one
on station.

Watch a harbor, a coastline, a shipping lane, a pipeline or a search area around the clock. A relief aircraft arrives before the one on station turns for home, so the picture never goes dark. Surveillance and awareness only: FREGATA carries no weapons.

Autonomous by default.
Yours when you want it.

It flies the plan. Takeoff, transition, the route, the orbit and the landing are flown by the aircraft. The crew watches, approves and steps in only when they choose to.

Point, and it goes. Tap a new orbit, a new heading or a new height. The aircraft works out how to get there safely and tells you before it does.

Hands on the sticks. A pilot can always take direct control. If the link drops, the aircraft takes back over, holds safe and heads for its planned recovery.

  • A fence it will not cross. The approved area is loaded before takeoff and checked many times a second.
  • A safe place to land. If something fails, it chooses the clearest spot within reach.
  • A motor out is not the end. Ten propellers share the load.
  • A small crew. The goal is 3 to 4 people per shift for the whole relay.

04 Field Kit

Two people.
No tools.

FREGATA travels in cases and comes apart into five modules, each light enough for two people to carry. Pins lock them, plugs mate blind, and every module tells the software who it is.

<30 minutes from cases to takeoff (target)

  1. Open the casesBody, left wing, right wing, tail and sensor pod.
  2. Click the modules togetherTool-free locking pins and blind-mate power and data plugs.
  3. Let the software checkEach module's ID, calibration and hours are read and verified. A copied or changed module is refused.
  4. Preflight goes green, lift offMotors, power, fence and link are all checked before the first propeller turns.
