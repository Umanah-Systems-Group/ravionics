A full dayon watch.

Ravionics lifts off a ship's deck or a small pad like a helicopter, then tilts its wing and flies like an airplane. It is designed to stay on station for 24 hours, with no runway and a small crew.

One sortie, in four phases

Tilt 90°Turning 10 of 10Power about 20 kW
01

Lift off a deck

Wing and tail point straight up. All ten propellers lift the aircraft off a pad or a moving deck, the battery helping the diesel through the peak.

02

Tilt into the wind

Wing and tail tilt forward together as speed builds, until the wing takes the weight. The flight software holds it steady the whole way.

03

Fold and cruise

Eight propellers fold flat against their pods. The two at the wingtips pull the aircraft along, and the diesel recharges the battery.

04

Loiter all day

On station it needs only a few kilowatts, a fraction of the hover power. That is what makes a full day in the air possible.

Inside

A hybrid heart.

Batteries alone cannot fly for a day, and engines alone cannot hover efficiently. Ravionics carries both, and the software decides which one works when. Pick a system to light it up.

From the box to the station

Two people.
No tools.

Ravionics travels in cases and comes apart into five modules, each light enough for two people to carry. Pins lock them, plugs mate blind, and every module tells the software who it is.

<30minutes, cases to takeoff (target)
  1. Open the casesBody, two wings, tail and payload pod.
  2. Click the modules togetherTool-free pins and blind-mate power and data plugs.
  3. Let the software checkEach module's ID, calibration and hours are read and verified. A copied or changed module is refused.
  4. Preflight goes green, lift offMotors, power, fence and link all checked before the first propeller turns.

The mission

Always one
on station.

Maritime domain awareness: watch a harbor, a coastline, a shipping lane or a search area around the clock. A relief aircraft arrives before the one on station turns for home, so the picture never goes dark.

People per shift is the crew goal. The aircraft flies itself; people supervise, decide and keep the record.

Deck

Takes off and lands vertically on a ship's deck or a small pad ashore. No runway, no catapult, no net.

Fleet

Planned to scale from one aircraft to hundreds, each one scheduled, relieved and maintained by the same software.

Control

Autonomous by default.
Yours when you want it.

It flies the plan.

Takeoff, transition, the route, the orbit and the landing are flown by the aircraft. The crew watches, approves and steps in only when they choose to.

Point, and it goes.

Tap a new orbit, a new heading or a new height. The aircraft works out how to get there safely and tells you before it does.

Hands on the sticks.

A pilot can always take direct control. If the link drops, the aircraft does not wait: it takes back over, holds safe and heads for its planned recovery.

  • A fence it will not cross. The approved area is loaded before takeoff and checked many times a second.
  • A safe place to land. If something fails, it chooses the clearest spot within reach.
  • A motor out is not the end. Ten propellers share the load, and the rest make up for one that stops.
